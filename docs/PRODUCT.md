# Aether: what it is, in simple words

## One sentence
**Make stars, planets and black holes with your bare hands, and watch them come alive.**

## Why this, and not Google Earth or Universe Sandbox
- **Google Earth / planetarium apps** let you *look* at space. You can't make anything.
- **Universe Sandbox / gravity simulators** let you build, but with menus, sliders and a mouse. It feels like software.
- **Aether** lets you **pull a star out of stardust with your fingers** and **throw a planet into orbit**. It should feel like magic, not like using a tool. It runs in a browser, with no install and nothing to learn.

## Who it is for
- Anyone who wants a "wow": kids, students, people who love space or love playing.
- People who want to record a 15-second clip of themselves making a solar system and share it.

---

## The 3 places

1. **Playground** (the heart, where you land first). Empty, beautiful space filled with soft stardust. You make whatever you want, customise what you make and how physics behaves, start from scenes, and share a link to your creation. Nothing can go wrong.
2. **Real Solar System.** Explore-only: our Sun, planets, Moon and spacecraft where they really are today, from NASA-based images and data. Click anything to learn about it; switch to true scale to feel how empty space is.
3. **The Universe** (to wander). Hundreds of galaxies; fly into any of them and create inside their star systems.

A small breadcrumb always shows where you are. Both open hands (or Esc) always take you back.

---

## How you create: a few gestures that feel natural

| You do | What happens |
|---|---|
| 🤏 **Pinch in empty space** | Stardust swirls into your fingertips and forms a glowing ball that grows while you hold |
| 🤏 **Let go quickly** | It becomes a **planet**. Move your hand as you let go to throw it; a glowing line previews its orbit before you release |
| 🤏 **Hold longer, then let go** | It becomes a **star**: the longer you hold, the bigger and bluer |
| 🤏 **Pinch something that exists** | Pick it up, move it, and throw it into a new orbit |
| ✌️ **Hold a peace sign** | A **black hole** forms where your hand is |
| 🤏🤏 **Pinch a star with both hands and pull apart** | It swells into a giant. Pull too far and it goes **supernova** |
| ✋ **Move an open hand** | Push stardust and gas like wind; turn the view |
| 👉 **Point and hold** | Go closer: fly into a galaxy or star system, or get a close-up of a planet |
| ✋✋ **Hold up both open hands** | Go back |

With a mouse, it's the same idea: **press and hold** = gather, **release** = create, **drag while releasing** = throw, **double-click** = go closer, **Esc** = back.

---

## What makes it feel like magic
- **Instant response:** stardust visibly flows toward your fingers the moment you pinch. You never wonder whether it's working.
- **Orbit preview:** before you let go, a soft line shows where your planet will travel. Throwing feels skilful, not random.
- **Gentle help:** a planet thrown roughly near a star settles into a beautiful orbit instead of crashing or flying off, unless you throw it hard.
- **Beautiful consequences:**
  - planets that collide merge with a flash;
  - black holes stretch things into glowing streams before swallowing them;
  - supernovae leave a nebula that slowly drifts.
- **Sound:** soft chimes when things are born, a deep hum near black holes, a boom for supernovae.
- **Smooth everywhere:** 60 fps, hands glide, nothing jumps, nothing triggers by accident.
- **One tap to share:** record a vertical clip of your creation, with you in it if you want.

## First minute (no reading needed)
1. You land in the Playground. One hint: **"Pinch and hold"**. Stardust swirls into your fingers.
2. You let go, and a star is born with a soft chime. Hint: **"Now pinch quickly near it and throw"**.
3. A planet settles into orbit around your star. You've made a solar system in under a minute.
4. Then the app suggests: try a peace sign (black hole), or visit the real Solar System.

## What "perfect" means
- A first-timer makes a star with an orbiting planet in **under 60 seconds** without reading anything.
- Every gesture shows feedback **immediately**.
- Nothing triggers by accident.
- It holds 60 fps on a normal laptop.
- People want to record it.

## Not doing (for now)
- Accounts and saving to the cloud (sharing a short link to your creation can come later).
- Realistic astrophysics accuracy over fun: it should *look* real, but play comes first.
